# Start here

A new Kimai installation does not require you to configure every feature before recording useful time.  The harder problem is deciding how the things you already do should map into Kimai's structure.

Before creating real customers or projects, spend a few minutes learning the model.  That small investment is useful because Kimai identifies its data structure and time-tracking mode as two of the choices that are comparatively expensive to change later.

The official [Initial setup](https://www.kimai.org/documentation/initial-setup.html) page provides a compact setup checklist.  This guide follows the same underlying product model, but slows down around the decisions a newcomer is otherwise expected to understand already.

## Your first three steps

1. Read [How Kimai thinks about your work](how-kimai-thinks.md).
2. Use [Plan your setup](plan-your-setup.md) to sketch your first customer, project, and activity structure before entering it.
3. Keep the first structure small.  Add complexity when you can name the problem it solves.

## What you do not need to decide yet

You do not need a complete organizational taxonomy before starting.  Depending on your circumstances, teams, custom roles, elaborate tags, budgets, custom fields, and other advanced features can wait.

Kimai itself describes teams as optional and notes that a small organization where everyone may see everything can skip them.  This guide will make similar distinctions throughout: features you need now, features you may need later, and features you can ignore until a concrete need appears.

## A useful target

A successful first configuration is not the one with the most options filled in.  It is the one where you can explain why a customer, project, and activity exist, record time without guessing, and understand how that time will later be billed or reported.
